# No One Home: story bible (v2)

**Why v2.** Two nights of play on a phone gave no fear and no sense of story. The first build scared with
sounds on a timer, and nothing the player did changed anything. Fear needs a rule the player learns, then sees
broken, and a question the player wants answered.

## The question
*Who is in the flat?* The player is told "someone" and shown only things a person could do: a door opened, a
light on, a chair moved, a tap running, a coat on the floor. Nothing floats, nothing is impossible.

## The truth (never stated, always inferable)
Martin walks in his sleep and does not remember it. He was found at 3 a.m. standing in the archive at work, eyes
open, not answering (the reason for "psychological problems"). Dr. Hale gave him five rules; he has stopped keeping
them. Every anomaly is him: bare dusty feet, his own coat, the chain only someone inside can take off, the phone
call out at 3:07 (the first thing the notebook shows is that it is always 3:07). He is ill (the cough in the flat is his)
and stops eating. Nobody asks after him. **Nobody finds him for 41 days.** That is the horror: not the figure, but
that nobody came. The ending never says "it was me": the figure turns, the face is his, and the epilogue shows the sofa.

## The rules of the flat (Dr. Hale's sheet, on the fridge)
1. Chain on the front door. 2. Bedroom door shut. 3. Hall light on. 4. Tablets with food. 5. Write down anything unusual.
Day 1 teaches them (the first task is to read the sheet, the last is to lock up). Then they are broken, one by one.
Rule 5 is the notebook: the NOTES button lists what Martin wrote down.

## A day
Wake (somewhere) → a thought, an OBJECTIVE → one to three tasks that walk him through the flat past what is
wrong → lock up (chain, door, light) → sleep → **3:07 a.m., awake in the dark**: a sound, go and check it, find
something, go back to bed → he wakes up elsewhere. From night 3 the hall light goes out as he steps into the hall.
Time in the dark builds dread; at full dread he loses time and the picture goes black. Light is safe: the figure
cannot stand in a lit room. Light switches glow in the dark.

## Day by day (changes must read at 360p, in the dark)
| Day | He wakes | What is out of place | Night (3:07) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | in bed | nothing (the routine, the sheet, the letter) | the bathroom tap is running |
| 2 | in bed | bedroom door open, kitchen light on | three knocks; peephole; chain still on |
| 3 | in bed | a chair outside his door, facing it | a chair scrapes: both dining chairs pulled out |
| 4 | standing at the hissing TV | grey bare footprints from his door | the phone is off the hook; last call out 3:07; the figure at the end of the hall |
| 5 | at the front door, hand on the chain | red coat on the floor; chain off | breathing at his door; the wardrobe emptied |
| 6 | in the storage room | Dad's boxes opened | the wall clock stopped at 3:07 |
| 7 | in the bathroom, feet wet | tub overflowing | Jack's bed is warm |
| 8 | in Jack's doorway | red coat folded on Jack's bed | the TV says his name |
| 9 | at the dining table | set for two; a note in pencil | the chain is off again |
| 10 | in bed | every light on, every door open, silence | (the figure waits in the living room) |

Jack's calls (days 4, 5, 7, 9; Martin answers 5, 7, 9) carry the human side: the more Martin admits, the less Jack can help.

## What the presence costs
Lost time. No health, no chase, no death. Time in the dark builds dread; at full dread the screen goes black and the
next morning he is somewhere he did not go to sleep. The notebook records it.

## Pacing
Beats are the data in `days.gd` (fear per day, tasks, scripted moments tied to progress, the night). Checks in the smoke
test: fear rises by one step per day, nights 1 and 2 teach with the light on and the rest are dark, every day ends with
locking up. Random events are gone; only rare ambient bits (a pipe, a neighbour) remain.
